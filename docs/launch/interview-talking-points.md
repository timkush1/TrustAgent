# Interview talking points

Eleven engineering stories from building TrustAgent 2.0, each told the same way: **the decision**,
**the alternatives** it beat, **the measured result**, and **what went wrong**. Each fits in about
two minutes; the links go to the code or the record behind it.

Say early how it was built: TrustAgent was designed and directed by one developer and built with AI
coding agents (Claude Code) to plans the developer approved and decisions the developer made; the
agents drafted the plans, wrote the code and the tests, and ran the reviews. Story 11 is how that
was run, and it is a strength to explain, not a footnote to hide.

Lead with the measured trade-off (story 1 and the calibration story, 4), then show the Studio (7).
Interviewers reward trade-off reasoning and measurement over feature counts.

**Honesty rule for these notes:** every figure below carries its label. Our own results come only
from the *Numbers to quote* table, which `make claims-write` regenerates from
`contracts/claims/measured.json`; until the measurement phase publishes a run, it says "not yet

## 1. Tiered triage, and why the gate is not the judge

- **Decision.** Three tiers on every answer: deterministic Go detectors (numbers, dates, URLs,
  quotes, citations, secrets), then one Jev call that asks every check at once, then a
  claim-level LLM audit only for flagged answers plus small samples: a response-level cascade,
  where the cheap check runs on everything and the expensive one only where it pays.
- **Alternatives.** An LLM judge on every answer: asking Claude Haiku the same questions costs
  <!-- claim:llm_same_checks_cost_per_1k --> ~$6 / 1,000 [estimate], against
  <!-- claim:fast_check_cost_per_1k --> ~$0.21 / 1,000 answers [estimate] for the fast check, and
  a full claim-level audit of every answer about
  <!-- claim:llm_full_audit_cost_per_1k --> ~$35 / 1,000 [estimate]. One call per check: cost and
  latency would grow with every check added.
- **Measured result.** The bench-lite Pareto chart (fast check, LLM same checks, full audit,
  cascade) with cost, balanced accuracy and latency, from RAGTruth-QA: the *agreement* and *cost
  ratio* rows above.
- **What went wrong.** The first design had the Python engine call Jev per claim as well as the
  gateway per answer: two clients, two sets of retries, caps and cost records. A design review cut
  it to one client in Go; the per-claim cascade waits for v2.1 behind a Go-hosted
  `DecisionService` ([ADR-005](../DECISIONS.md#adr-005-jev-is-the-tier-1-check-on-every-answer-behind-a-vendor-neutral-decisionmodel)).

## 2. Hot-path engineering

- **Decision.** A deadline budget per request; detached attempts with capacity guards; hedging; a
  circuit breaker that counts only vendor faults, never our own cancellations; fail-open by
  default and fail-closed per route; late decisions that are recorded but never enforced.
- **Alternatives.** A plain client timeout with retries (a hung vendor eats the whole budget); a
  breaker that counts every failed attempt (our own cancelled hedges would open it under load).
- **Measured result.** The k6 run against simulated vendors: the *proxy overhead* rows above, per
  mode in [GATEWAY.md](../GATEWAY.md#13-load-test). The fault pack replays every vendor failure
  (rate limits, 503s, hangs, out of credits, bad JSON, oversized requests) and checks the
  action each one must produce.
- **What went wrong.** A design review found that a fail-closed route could be bypassed: when the
  daily budget ran out, the check was "skipped", and skipped did not count as an error. Now it
  does on a closed route, and enforcing routes keep a reserve of the daily decision budget.

## 3. Honest decision semantics

- **Decision.** No evidence means `not_assessed`, never a guess. Blocking on groundedness needs
  two agreeing signals, and "nothing supports it" is kept apart from "something contradicts it"
  (CONTRADICTED vs UNSUPPORTED, end to end). New checks start in shadow. A route that cannot
  block yet acts as flag and says why, with the fix.
- **Alternatives.** Score every answer anyway; block on one probability; downgrade silently.
- **Measured result.** A public test of Jev (Haqq, on legal citations:
  https://www.haqq.ai/blog/jev-legal-ai-hallucination-detection) found that without source
  documents its groundedness ranking inverts (fabricated citations scored above honest ones); that
  third-party finding is why groundedness is never asked without evidence.
- **What went wrong.** In 1.0, every claim without context came back UNSUPPORTED, which read as
  "the model made it up". The engine now checks for evidence first and marks such claims
  NOT_CHECKED.

## 4. Calibration done right

- **Decision.** Raw and calibrated threshold spaces are separate, and a calibration changes
  decisions only when adopted through a policy version. Quality counts as measured only from
  human or public labels; proxy labels can never unlock blocking. The critical threshold uses the
  Wilson lower bound.
- **Alternatives.** Ship the vendor's 0.5 default; fit on the judge's labels; fit once and forget
  the snapshot.
- **Measured result.** The 0.5 default is wrong: a third-party test reached
  <!-- claim:arize_ragtruth_accuracy_at_default_threshold --> 76% at 0.5 [third-party] against
  <!-- claim:arize_ragtruth_jev_accuracy --> 87% [third-party] at its best, which shows why
  thresholds must be calibrated per question and snapshot. That best was reached only at a tuned
  0.80 threshold on a differently framed question; possible training contamination. Not directly
  transferable to our questions. Our own calibration error, before and after, held out on the
  bench's test sample, is the "groundedness calibration error (ECE)" row of *Numbers to quote* once
  a run is published; until then it is not measured yet, and so say it.
- **What went wrong.** Labels carry no route, so a calibration fit filtered by route loses the
  labels whose traces retention already deleted. Documented as a known gap for v2.1.

## 5. Unbiased measurement

- **Decision.** The headline hallucination rate comes only from a uniform Bernoulli sample taken
  before the gate, shown with n and a Wilson confidence interval, and hidden below n = 30. The share
  among audited answers is labelled as biased.
- **Alternatives.** Report the rate among audited answers (they are audited because they looked
  risky, so the rate is inflated by design).
- **Measured result.** The Savings page shows measured spend next to two labelled
  counterfactuals, and the rate only with its interval.
- **What went wrong.** In the zero-key demo, the mock judge costs nothing, so the "full LLM audit"
  counterfactual averaged to zero. Free simulated audits now fall back to the published cost
  assumptions, labelled estimate.

## 6. Cross-language determinism

- **Decision.** One Go reference for every decision, mechanical vectors generated from it and
  frozen after review, and a TypeScript conformer that runs the same decisions in the browser
  (the static site's live checks).
- **Alternatives.** Port the logic and hope; call the gateway from the static site.
- **Measured result.** Every frozen golden vector passes in Go and in TypeScript on every CI run.
- **What went wrong.** The traps are real: canonical JSON numbers, U+2028 in strings, the order of
  float sums, UTF-16 offsets, RE2 and JavaScript regex differences. And a hand-written PKCE vector
  pair in the plan turned out to be garbled; the RFC 7636 vectors caught it before any code
  shipped.

## 7. Evaluator Studio

- **Decision.** An LLM (System 2) writes the check once; Jev (System 1) runs it on every request.
  A draft costs <!-- claim:studio_draft_cost_estimate --> ~$0.005 per draft [estimate];
  running it costs <!-- claim:fast_check_cost_per_check --> ~$0.0002 per check [estimate].
  Every draft is validated by a schema and lints, tested on samples, dry-run on past traffic,
  watched in shadow and calibrated before it can act.
- **Alternatives.** Hand-write classifier prompts; run the LLM on every request.
- **Measured result.** The dry-run reports how often a check would have fired on real past
  traffic and what that run cost, before anyone turns it on.
- **What went wrong.** "Draft with AI" said "engine unavailable" when the real cause was a refused
  key or an empty account. The engine and gateway now pass typed errors (`judge_unauthorized`,
  `no_credits`) through to words that say what to fix.

## 8. Security

- **Decision.** Self-hosted PKCE with the verifier held by the server; keys sealed with
  AES-256-GCM, authenticated by name and provider, bound to their hosts; a `content` scope for
  bodies; the setup token in the URL fragment; a browser-only key flow for the public demo, with
  a CSP and a test that the site contacts nothing but itself and openrouter.ai.
- **Alternatives.** Env-only secrets (no one-click connect); keys in the browser for the dashboard.
- **Measured result.** Leak tests run with a secret no pattern matches and assert it never reaches
  a log, an error, the API, the stream or the page.
- **What went wrong.** In 1.0 the gateway read its own key from `Authorization`, the header the
  provider SDKs fill with the upstream key (bug B1). Integration testing also found that dial-time
  checks had to happen after DNS (to refuse link-local and cloud-metadata addresses), and that a
  vendor message cut across an echoed key could leave a prefix of it in an error.

## 9. Bugs found and fixed

- **B5:** the WebSocket pump joined messages into one frame. **B6:** a race on the hub's client
  map. **B4:** a repeated client id appended claims to another audit's history. **B2:** a
  compressed upstream body slipped past the check.
- **D1:** an answer with zero claims crashed the scorer. **D3:** CONTRADICTED scored the same as
  UNSUPPORTED. **D4:** a judge outage produced a normal-looking score. **M7:** test mode was always
  on.
- How each was found and fixed is traceable in SPEC Appendix C and the CHANGELOG.

## 10. Limits and next steps

- **Limits** (the same five the landing page lists): Jev launched on an alpha endpoint with no
  published calibration, so thresholds are calibrated locally per version; it is weak on numbers,
  dates and negation (detectors and the deep audit cover them); groundedness needs documents;
  English first; not a security boundary.
- **Next.** The per-claim cascade inside the deep audit behind a Go `DecisionService`. The
  vendor-neutral `DecisionModel` answers the skeptic's "a small classifier or logprobs could do
  this": the backend is swappable, and the benchmark table decides.

## 11. Running gated agent streams

- **Decision.** The code was written by AI coding agents (Claude Code), directed like a team: each
  phase had a written plan with numbered, binding decisions; agent streams worked in separate git
  worktrees and branches with file ownership; a stream merged only after its gate passed (`make
  check`, the golden vectors, the contract checks, the gating scanners); separate review agents
  then checked correctness, coverage and design, with fix rounds until they passed. The owner made
  the product decisions, approved each phase plan and authorized the pushes and every paid run
  (each one a capped dry run until confirmed); agents merged after their gates and the review
  agents passed; `main` now changes only through pull requests the owner merges.
- **Alternatives.** One long chat session (no memory between days, no parallel work); agents that
  push straight to the main branch; reviewing only by reading the diff.
- **Measured result.** Cross-language decision logic proven by golden vectors rather than review,
  the gating scanners (gosec, bandit, npm audit, gitleaks) clean at every merge, and every paid
  step a dry run unless confirmed (the plans in `docs/v2/` and the merge commits are the record).
- **What went wrong.** Two runs of the same fix round once started in parallel, and their agents
  worked in one worktree until one was killed for memory: the rule became "stop the old run before
  relaunching". A review found that a run's "measured" label was decided by the backend's name, so
  output from a mock vendor could have been labelled measured; the fix made the vendor host part of
  the evidence (D203). Trivy, an informational scanner outside the merge gates, went red on `main`
  once v2 was pushed there (vulnerable Go and Python dependencies, and the detector vectors' fake
  keys); the fix (the Go 1.26 toolchain, gRPC 1.84, LangGraph 1.x, a secret allow-list for those
  vectors only) came after the push, so the lesson was to read the informational jobs before a
  push too. And the plans assumed one laptop until the work moved to a cloud session mid-way, so
  the owner guides were rewritten for it.

## What is most impressive, and do people value it?

- **Not "integrated Jev".** It is:
  1. a cost, accuracy and latency trade-off measured on our own benchmark, with calibration error
     and confidence intervals: the harness is built, but say "measured" only after a published
     benchmark run (today every measured row says "not yet measured");
  2. a fail-safe hot path whose overhead is published once the release load run is in
     `measured.json` (the gateway's Tier-0 timings are already published, measured on the dev
     laptop);
  3. decision logic identical in Go and in the browser, proven by golden vectors;
  4. honesty built into the product: every number labelled, and "not checked" instead of an
     invented score.
